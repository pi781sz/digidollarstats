What is DigiDollar?

DigiDollar ($DD) is a decentralized US-dollar stablecoin built directly into the DigiByte blockchain. You create it by locking your own DGB as collateral — there is no bank, no company, and you keep control of your private keys the entire time.

Lock DGB as collateral and the protocol mints $DD worth one dollar each. Later, burn the $DD to unlock your DGB. It's fully reversible — and your coins never leave your wallet.

The short version

DigiDollar lets you draw stable, dollar-denominated spending power from DGB you already hold — without selling it, and without handing your coins to anyone. You lock DGB, mint $DD, spend or hold it, and later return the $DD to release your DGB. You're borrowing against your own coins, not selling them.

Unlike stablecoins backed by a company's bank account, DigiDollar has no issuer to trust. It's enforced by DigiByte's own transaction rules — a stablecoin native to the chain, not a smart contract, a bridge, or an ERC-20 token layered on top.

How it works

Four steps, all happening inside DigiByte Core:

1

Lock your DGB

You commit DGB into a time-locked output in your own wallet. It's locked, not sent — it stays under your keys the whole time.

2

Mint DigiDollar

Based on the value of the DGB you locked and the current DGB/USD price, the protocol mints $DD to you. Each one is worth a dollar.

3

Use it

Send, receive, or hold $DD like any DigiByte transaction — but with a steady dollar value instead of a floating one.

4

Redeem

When your lock period ends, burn the $DD to release your collateral. Your DGB unlocks in full and returns to you.

What makes it different

  • Genuinely decentralized. No issuer, no custodian, no bank behind it — it lives in the DigiByte protocol itself.
  • Self-custody throughout. Your DGB never leaves your wallet. You hold your own keys from start to finish.
  • No forced liquidations. Because collateral is time-locked, positions are never margin-called. They ride out the term.
  • Native to a UTXO chain. Built into DigiByte's transaction rules using Taproot outputs — not layered on via contracts.

Collateral & lock periods

DigiDollar is over-collateralized: you always lock more DGB than the dollars you mint. How much more depends on how long you lock — shorter locks require more collateral, longer locks require less. There are ten tiers, from one hour to ten years.

How the price stays near a dollar

DigiDollar reads the DGB/USD price from a decentralized oracle network — up to 35 independent oracle slots that draw from major exchange feeds. A quorum of seven signs each price bundle onto the chain using MuSig2 aggregate signatures, so no single oracle sets the price. You can watch the feed live on Oracle Watch.

What keeps the system stable

With no liquidations to fall back on, DigiDollar relies on layered safeguards instead:

  • Over-collateralization. Every position starts with a buffer against DGB price drops — larger for short locks.
  • Dynamic adjustment. Collateral requirements tighten automatically if overall system health falls.
  • Emergency redemption. Under stress, redeeming can require burning a little more $DD — but you always get 100% of your collateral back.
  • Volatility freezes. Minting can pause during extreme price moves to protect the system, then resume.
The honest part

DigiDollar is liquidity against your own locked DGB — not free money. The debt is fixed in US dollars, and time-locks have no early exit: once you lock, that DGB is committed for the full term. Decide carefully. Nothing here is financial advice.

Where things stand

DigiDollar is live on DigiByte mainnet, shipped in DigiByte Core v9.26.2. Everything — minting, holding, redeeming, and the oracle price feed — runs inside DigiByte Core, with no third-party service required.

Frequently asked

Is DigiDollar a real stablecoin?
Yes. Each $DD targets a value of one US dollar and is backed by DGB that's been over-collateralized and locked on-chain.
Who controls DigiDollar?
No one. There's no issuing company or custodian — it's part of the DigiByte protocol and runs on self-custodied wallets.
Do I lose my DGB when I mint DigiDollar?
No. Your DGB is time-locked inside your own wallet and returns to you in full when you redeem.
Can my position be liquidated?
No. Collateral is time-locked, so there are no margin calls or forced liquidations. A position simply rides out its full term.
How is the price kept at a dollar?
A decentralized network of up to 35 oracle slots reports DGB/USD, and a 7-signature MuSig2 quorum signs each price onto the chain. See Oracle Watch.
Where does DigiDollar run?
Entirely inside DigiByte Core — the same wallet you already use for DGB. There's nothing extra to sign up for. New to nodes? See Run a Node.
